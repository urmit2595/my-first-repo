**Squad Surge engine audit — 7 November 2026**

Static review of the five requested files; phone performance estimates below are hypotheses, not benchmarks. Citations use repository paths and source line numbers.

**Architecture today.** `index.html` owns mutable level state, input, simulation, rendering and HUD; modules share `ctx` and synchronous bus events (`index.html:400–425`). Each animation frame runs at most one variable simulation step, clamps elapsed time to 50 ms, applies hit-stop/slow-motion, then renders. There is no accumulator or interpolation; long stalls discard elapsed time (`index.html:877–893,1349–1360`). Powerups simulate on `tick`; Juice changes camera and presentation on `frame` (`js/powerups.js:299–326`; `js/juice.js:468–509`).

Soldiers, enemies and boss are atlas-animated, camera-facing quads using custom `InstancedBufferGeometry`, not individual THREE.Sprites or volumetric characters. Bullets/effects share an additive instanced layer; ground shadows use `InstancedMesh`. Thus the core has up to six nonempty batch draws: soldiers, ordinary enemies, brutes, boss, effects and shadows (`index.html:278–364,1280–1318`). Scenery merges buildings into 90-unit chunks with up to three material meshes, and instances prop billboards/shadows (`js/scenery.js:486–489,561–568,671–700`). Gates/solids are separate mesh assemblies sharing resources: an ordinary gate has eleven component meshes before optional decorations; labels have individual canvas textures. Distance culling limits visible objects, but does not batch them (`js/objects3d.js:299–325,391–452,545–558`). Total draws depend on visible scenery, objects, effects and shadow passes.

Formation uses golden-angle spiral slots, smoothed toward a moving center, with individual X positions clamped to road edges. There is no soldier separation or obstacle avoidance (`index.html:728–734,965–1002`). Gates select one half using the center; solids trigger when the center crosses their Z, count soldiers in an X column, remove casualties and break. Enemies collide with an approximate squad ellipse, not individuals (`index.html:1004–1031,1083–1104`).

Autofire samples random soldiers; at most 48 effective shooters emit projectiles, with damage multiplied by squad/shooter count. Weapons vary rate, spread and splash; SURGE adds piercing. Bullets move at 72 units/s with 46-unit lifetime range. Collision uses swept Z crossings plus X bounds, testing solids, gates, enemies, then boss; array order/category priority can override the nearest physical hit (`index.html:209–239,1033–1079`).

Levels are generated upfront: a hardcoded tutorial, then seeded selections from repeated segment templates, reserved threat corridors and supplies; length caps at 520. This is procedural composition, not streamed segments or a story-level script (`index.html:632–724`; `js/powerups.js:149–159`). The boss approaches, tracks X until its warning, repeatedly slams a radius-three area, accelerates attacks below half HP, and wipes the squad on contact; death hands off to the finale (`index.html:426–431,942–944,1106–1129,1172–1186`).

**Hot spots and limits.** With B bullets and nearby E enemies, O solids and G gates, collision costs O(B(E+O+G)), after full-array filters. This is potentially quadratic as populations grow, not an all-pairs crowd solver. Explosions scan whole populations; removing K nearby soldiers repeatedly searches and splices N soldiers, O(KN), worst-case O(N²) (`index.html:739–746,935–938,1051–1079,1223–1229`). Dead enemies remain in arrays and are revisited (`index.html:1084–1104,1290–1297`).

Allocation pressure includes one slot array per soldier per step, candidate filters, projectile/FX objects and splice results. Instance buffers are marked dirty every render. HUD text, attributes, classes, widths and positions are repeatedly written; Juice's subtree `hidden` observer can amplify these into modal queries (`index.html:350–364,444–459,998–1001,1039–1053,1133–1139,1329–1345`; `js/juice.js:322–340`). Hit labels redraw/upload textures when numbers change (`js/objects3d.js:285–291,454–457`). Existing pooling helps: 56 debris meshes, ten rings and eight bombs; these still draw separately (`js/objects3d.js:470–475`; `js/powerups.js:55–63`).

The 600-soldier cap is explicit, but its rationale is undocumented. It plausibly bounds CPU work, visual density and gameplay growth; it is not a renderer limit—the soldier buffer holds 700. Damage still scales past 48 shooters (`index.html:732,1037–1044,1374–1381`). At 600, formation radius is approximately 12.6 on a road only 12 wide, collapsing many slots against boundaries and restricting center steering to ±0.6. Formation readability/control breaks before another hundred instances would necessarily exhaust graphics (`index.html:209,728–729,971–1001`). Buffer overflow silently drops visuals, including effects beyond 1,400 (`index.html:343–344,1379`).

On a mid-range phone, likely first pressures are GPU fill/shadows and unbatched objects in decorated scenes, then collision/GC spikes during dense volleys and explosions. High quality uses up to 2× pixel ratio and a moving 1024² shadow map; adaptive quality disables shadows and lowers resolution, but cannot fix simulation complexity (`index.html:1244–1266`; `js/scenery.js:239–243,704–714`). Measure frame-time percentiles, allocation rate and renderer draw counts before claiming a specific FPS limit.

**Why play repeats.** Zombies/grunts differ mainly in speed; brutes add HP, size and contact damage. All walk forward and drift toward squad X: no ordinary enemy shooting, flanking, directional shielding or telegraphed charge. Mortar towers do fire targeted shells; swarms run faster and resist bullets (`index.html:538–550,579–605,627–630,1081–1104,1158–1160`). Gaps do not reshape the crowd. Difficulty mostly increases HP/counts/rewards and boss HP, rather than tactical combinations. Guaranteed opening boosts, supplied counters, recovery corridors and threat loss caps protect snowballing squads; ordinary threat damage preserves at least 60% of starting strength or three soldiers (`index.html:529–550,628,639–647,668–713,722`; `js/powerups.js:149–157,308–312`).

**Prioritised implementation plan.** Effort estimates are engineering days for one developer, excluding art and campaign production.

1. **Fixed timestep and baseline profiling — 3–5 days; high reliability impact.** Add a 60 Hz accumulator, bounded catch-up, render interpolation and explicit pause/slow-motion clocks. Move authoritative changes out of presentation callbacks. Record representative phone traces before/after.
2. **Deterministic replay/test mode — 4–6 days; high regression impact.** Extend `__game` (`index.html:1395–1396`): record tick-indexed inputs, level/version, seed and initial save-dependent settings; hash authoritative state. Replace gameplay `Math.random` and wall-clock dependencies with separate seeded streams/tick clocks (`index.html:630,638,880–886,1041`; `js/powerups.js:212`). Test identical results across render rates, gate crossings, splash chains and boss transitions.
3. **Authored campaign interpreter — 5–8 days; highest variety impact.** Add validated JSON with stable event IDs, distance/time/condition triggers, ordered execution, objectives and checkpoints. Example record shapes:

   ```json
   {"version":1,"id":"bridge-02","theme":"bridge","length":320,
    "events":[
      {"id":"intro","atDistance":0,"event":"dialogue","text":"Hold the bridge"},
      {"id":"wave1","atDistance":40,"wave":{"type":"rifleman","count":8,"lane":-1}},
      {"id":"haz1","atDistance":90,"hazard":{"type":"crusher","period":3}},
      {"id":"gate1","atDistance":120,"gate":{"left":["+",20],"right":["x",2]}},
      {"id":"boss1","atDistance":300,"boss":{"type":"ogre","script":"bridge-ogre"}}]}
   ```

4. **Enemy/boss behavior scripts — 8–12 days; high tactical impact.** Implement telegraphed rifle bursts, flank waypoints, frontal shields and wind-up charges. Boss phase data specifies HP thresholds, attack sequences, summons and recovery windows; validate reachable dodges. Extend the existing factories and boss loop cited above.
5. **Crowd/collision upgrade — 10–15 days; high fairness/scaling impact.** Introduce spatial hashing for swept bullet queries and bounded local neighbors; resolve nearest hits. Add velocity-based cohesion/separation, obstacle look-ahead, gap-dependent formation width/depth and per-unit contacts. Give deaths pooled knockback visuals. Stress-test 600 units at chokepoints; dense hash cells still need work limits.
6. **Rendering/allocation pass — 5–8 days; high phone impact.** Preserve existing unit instancing; use `InstancedMesh` for units only if adopting actual 3D models. Batch repeated solid parts/debris, atlas labels, chunk/cull props, add distance LOD/shadow budgets, pool bullets/FX, cache slots and update HUD only on changes.
7. **Dynamic difficulty hooks — 3–5 days; medium pacing impact.** Feed retention, damage, clear time and counter usage into bounded adjustments between encounters: wave budget, recovery spacing and supplies. Preserve telegraphs; record decisions in replays and disable adaptation for competitive seeds. Existing tactics/outcome events provide inputs (`index.html:770,1220,1241`).
